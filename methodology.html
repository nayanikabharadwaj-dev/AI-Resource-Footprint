How the estimate works

No AI company publishes per-message energy or water figures for Claude. Every number this extension shows is an estimate based on the most recent public measurements of comparable AI models.

The reference exchange

One "typical" exchange is taken as about 600 tokens: a short question and an answer of roughly 500 tokens, the answer length Epoch AI assumed in its 2025 estimate. Each real exchange is scaled by its length relative to that, with length estimated as characters ÷ 4.

Electricity: 0.3 Wh per typical exchange

The centre of three 2025 figures that agree closely: Google's measured median Gemini Apps text prompt (0.24 Wh, Measuring the environmental impact of AI inference, Aug 2025), Epoch AI's estimate for GPT-4o (~0.3 Wh, Feb 2025) and OpenAI's stated average ChatGPT query (0.34 Wh, Jun 2025). An earlier and widely quoted figure of about 3 Wh (de Vries, Joule, 2023) is now considered roughly ten times too high for a short query.

Water: about 1.1 mL per typical exchange

Calculated as 3.7 mL per Wh, from Li et al., Making AI Less "Thirsty" (2023, updated 2025): about 0.55 L/kWh for on-site data-centre cooling plus about 3.14 L/kWh of off-site water used to generate the electricity (US average). Google's own figure of 0.26 mL per prompt counts cooling only; this extension includes power generation too, so it reads about four times higher.

What the estimate leaves out

Training the model, manufacturing the hardware, network traffic and your own device are not included. Each turn of a long conversation also reprocesses earlier messages, and reasoning models use more energy per answer, so long or complex conversations are likely underestimated. Actual figures for Claude depend on hardware, data-centre location and model, none of which are public.

If each prompt is small, why is AI's footprint so large?

Both are true. The figures here describe one person's short text chats, which really are small. AI's total footprint is large for other reasons:

  • Scale. Fractions of a watt-hour, repeated billions of times a day across every AI service, add up to a meaningful amount of electricity.
  • Chat is only part of it. Data centres also run image and video generation, reasoning models, long-running coding agents, business use through APIs, and recommendation and search systems. Training each new model is a large extra cost. None of this is in a per-prompt figure.
  • Growth. The International Energy Agency estimates data centres used about 415 TWh of electricity in 2024 (around 1.5% of the world's total) and projects this roughly doubling to about 945 TWh by 2030, with AI the main driver.
  • Local impact. A data centre's water and power use is concentrated in one place. In a drought-stressed area or on a strained grid, it matters to that community even when it is small globally.
  • Rebound effect. As AI gets more efficient per prompt, it gets cheaper and is used more, so total consumption can keep rising.
  • Self-reported data. The per-prompt figures come from the companies themselves, who decide what to count.

Comparisons

LED bulb: 10 W. Phone charge: about 12 Wh. Teaspoon: 5 mL.

Privacy

The extension reads the text of your claude.ai messages only to count characters. Everything is calculated in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere, and only the session totals (character-based token counts and number of exchanges) are stored locally.
